02Case studies

What this looks like in practice

Six problems, and what came out the other side.

001

A mobile app for people with clay on their hands

Zero to one in build

Independent · Founder

My own product, built from nothing: a workshop journal for ceramists, with a project log, a pricing calculator that accounts for what a firing actually costs, and a forum for trading glaze recipes. The roadmap is organized around retention and word of mouth, because for a niche audience nothing else compounds.

002

A spreadsheet ritual, turned into a product

Planning cycle −30%

Disney · Ad forecasting

Ad inventory planning ran on spreadsheets and tribal knowledge, so the answer depended on who you asked. I led development of a forecasting product that made planning reproducible and became the basis for how inventory gets monetized.

003

Teaching the forecast to behave like the ad server

Accuracy +11%

Disney · Delivery modelling

Models fit to historical curves looked excellent in a backtest and under-delivered in production. I spearheaded a proprietary model that mirrors how ad serving actually behaves instead of averaging what it did before, which cut the under-delivery we had to make good on.

004

Two hundred vendor feeds, one pipeline

Annual cost −42%

Disney · Reporting

Reporting depended on a sprawl of vendor exports that no two teams reconciled the same way. I launched the third-party platform STAQ with more than 200 API connections behind it, which left one number everyone could point at.

005

Launching Disney+ in two hemispheres

Launch day reporting

Disney · LATAM and ANZ

The LATAM and ANZ launches needed working reporting on day one, not in the quarter after. I managed the data pipelines underneath them, which is what made funnel optimization and global reporting possible across the Direct and Programmatic businesses.

006

The churn hiding in the billing logic

Billing churn resolved

Disney · Subscription

Subscribers who bought more than one Disney service were being double- and split-billed, and they were leaving over it. I worked with engineering and finance to trace and resolve it, then dug into Verizon upgrade and downgrade behavior in a way that changed both campaign strategy and which partnerships were worth renewing.

03AI builds

Shipped when eng resources weren’t available

Four gaps that needed filling now — scoped, built, and shipped without a roadmap slot, using Cursor, Claude, and whatever else was lying around.

001

A sell-through dashboard when eng wasn’t scheduled

Report pull time −90%

Disney · Competitive inventory

Revenue teams needed sell-through on competitive inventory, but there was no engineering slot for a real dashboard. I built a Confluence pseudo-dashboard wired to live data — SQL and layout with Cursor doing the heavy lifting — so stakeholders check it weekly instead of pulling reports by hand. Some manual cleanup still lands on them; even so, time spent gathering the number dropped by about ninety percent.

002

Catching audience drop-offs before they become zero forecasts

Zero surprises → daily catch

Disney · Audience coverage

When audience segments fell off, stakeholders usually found out from a zero in the forecast and asked us to investigate. I built a coverage tracking dashboard and automated a daily send so the drop surfaces the morning it happens, not when someone stumbles on it mid-plan.

003

Planning poker after Jira took it away

Estimation restored

Disney · Databricks

Jira Cloud dropped blind planning poker, and the team still needed it for estimation. I built a replacement on Databricks with Cursor — a small app ten to fifteen engineers run in refinement again, the way we did before the feature disappeared.

004

Mockups engineering is building from now

Discovery −40+ hrs

Disney · Forecasting platform

The standalone forecasting platform needed a direction engineering could build without another round of whiteboard debate. I used AI to produce interactive mocks and specs tight enough that stakeholders aligned faster; engineering is actively building from them now, and discovery time dropped by forty-plus hours versus how we scoped features before.
