03Work

What this looks like in practice.

Six problems I was handed, and what came out the other side.

001

A spreadsheet ritual, turned into a product

Disney

Ad inventory planning ran on spreadsheets and tribal knowledge, so the answer depended on who you asked. I led development of a forecasting product that made planning reproducible and became the basis for how inventory gets monetized.

Planning cycle −30%

002

Teaching the forecast to behave like the ad server

Disney

Models fit to historical curves looked excellent in a backtest and under-delivered in production. I spearheaded a proprietary model that mirrors how ad serving actually behaves instead of averaging what it did before, which cut the under-delivery we had to make good on.

Accuracy +11%

003

Two hundred vendor feeds, one pipeline

Disney

Reporting depended on a sprawl of vendor exports that no two teams reconciled the same way. I launched the third-party platform STAQ with more than 200 API connections behind it, which left one number everyone could point at.

Annual cost −42%

004

Launching Disney+ in two hemispheres

Disney

The LATAM and ANZ launches needed working reporting on day one, not in the quarter after. I managed the data pipelines underneath them, which is what made funnel optimization and global reporting possible across the Direct and Programmatic businesses.

Two regions live at launch

005

The churn hiding in the billing logic

Disney

Subscribers who bought more than one Disney service were being double- and split-billed, and they were leaving over it. I worked with engineering and finance to trace and resolve it, then dug into Verizon upgrade and downgrade behavior in a way that changed both campaign strategy and which partnerships were worth renewing.

Billing churn resolved

A workshop journal for people with clay on their hands

Founder

Zero to one in progress

04Method

Opinions I picked up the hard way.

Ten years of shipping into ad systems leaves you with a few convictions.

01

Numbers people will bet on

A forecast is only worth building if the person staking revenue on it believes it. Accuracy is table stakes; trust is the actual product.

02

Model the system, not the average

Historical curves flatter themselves in a backtest. Mirroring how the machine really behaves is what survives contact with production.

03

The pipeline is the product

A dashboard is the visible tenth. Whether anyone trusts it was decided upstream, in the plumbing nobody wanted to own.

04

Test instead of arguing

An A/B test settles in two weeks what a roadmap debate will not settle in two quarters, and it settles it with evidence.

05

Revenue hides in unglamorous places

Double billing, floor prices, vendor feeds. The boring seams are where the money and the churn both turn out to live.

06

Stay through the first versions

How people use a thing is never quite what the spec said, and that gap is only visible to whoever is still paying attention after launch.
